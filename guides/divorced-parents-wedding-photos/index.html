Divorced parents in wedding family photos: an order that keeps everyone comfortable

You don't have to choose between a tense photo and no photo. Take one photo with each parent, let a new partner stand with the parent they came with, and plan the order so the two of them never share a frame.

The rule: one photo with each parent, not one with both

If your parents are divorced and would rather not stand side by side, every group that would put them together becomes two groups. "Couple with Emma's parents" becomes "Couple with Emma's parents, with Carol" and "Couple with Emma's parents, with Rick and Linda". Everyone else in the group (grandparents, a sister, her husband and kids) stays in both versions. You lose nothing; you gain a second frame.

A new husband or wife stands with the parent they came with.

If your parents get on fine and both want a photo together, skip all of this. Ask each of them on their own, not in a group chat, and plan around the answer.

An order for one divorced pair

Here is the 11-photo order the planner produced for the smallest family above: Emma's parents Carol and Rick are divorced, James's parents Diane and Paul are married, and each of them has one sibling, Kate and Chris.

  1. Couple with James's parents and ChrisChris is free to go.
  2. Couple with James's parents
  3. James with his parentsEmma steps out for one frame.
  4. Both sets of parents, with Rick
  5. Both sets of parents, with CarolRick steps out, Carol steps in. Diane and Paul are free to go.
  6. Couple with Carol and Kate
  7. Couple with Rick and KateOne swap again. Kate is free to go.
  8. Couple with Rick
  9. Emma with RickRick is free to go.
  10. Emma with Carol
  11. Couple with CarolCarol is free to go. Done by 4:17 PM if you start at 4:00.

Three things make it work. The side without the divorce goes first and is done in five photos. The "with Carol" and "with Rick" versions of each group are taken back to back, so every swap is one parent out and one parent in while the rest of the group holds its pose. And the photos with just the couple and one parent come last, when the crowd has gone.

If a parent has remarried, keep the new partner apart too

The easy mistake is to mark only the two exes. Say Diane and Paul are divorced and Diane has married Greg. You tell the planner Diane never stands next to Paul, but Greg has no rule, so nothing stops him landing in Paul's photos. When we tried exactly that, "James with parents, with Paul" came out as Greg, Paul and James.

The fix is a second pair: Greg never stands next to Paul. Then Paul's photos are Paul and the couple, and Diane's are Diane, Greg and the couple. The planner's example family does the same thing on the other side: Carol is kept apart from both Rick and his wife Linda, so Rick and Linda always appear together and Carol has her own frames.

When both sides are divorced

"Both sets of parents" is the photo that grows fastest. With two divorced pairs it becomes four photos, one for every pairing: Carol with Paul, Rick with Paul, Rick with Diane and Greg, Carol with Diane and Greg. That is why the 8-relative family above jumps from 9 photos to 19.

Many couples don't need all four. Keep the pairings that matter to you, untick the rest, and press "Put them in the best order" again. Dropping two of the four takes that family to 17 photos, about 26 minutes.

On the day

  • Tell each parent their photo numbers in advance. A text the week before saying "you're in photos 5, 6, 10 and 11, from about 4:06" means nobody hovers, and nobody is surprised by who else is waiting.
  • Keep the waiting parent off to one side. Whoever calls names should bring the next parent in from wherever they are waiting, not from inside the group being photographed. The call cards say who to call next; see who should call names.
  • Each parent stands next to their own child. A new partner stands on the outside of the parent they came with.
  • If one parent needs to leave early, tick "needs to go first" next to their name. The planner moves their photos up and still keeps them apart from the other parent.

Questions

Do divorced parents have to be in a photo together at the wedding?

No. The usual way around it is one photo with each parent instead of one with both. Every group they would share, such as the couple with parents and siblings, is taken twice, once with each parent. If they get along and both want a photo together, take one.

Where should a step-parent stand in wedding family photos?

Next to the parent they came with, on that parent's outside, so each parent stands beside their own child. If you keep divorced parents apart, add the step-parent to the keep-apart list too, or they can end up in the other parent's photo.

How much longer do family photos take with divorced parents?

In the Family Formals planner, keeping one divorced pair apart added 4 to 6 photos, about 6 to 9 minutes at 1.5 minutes per photo. With both sets of parents divorced, an 8-person family went from 9 photos to 19, from about 14 minutes to 29.

What if my divorced parents get along?

Then don't keep them apart. Ask each of them privately first. If both are happy to stand together, you get the shorter list: in our smallest example, 7 photos instead of 11.
