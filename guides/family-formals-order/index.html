The best order for family formals

Start with the biggest group and take people away. Finish one family before the other. Photograph grandparents and small children early. Here's why, with the 13-photo order the planner produced for a 16-person family.

Three rules that decide the order

  1. Start with the biggest group, then take people away.Everyone in the big group is needed once. Shoot it first and the aunts, uncles and cousins are done in two minutes.
  2. Finish one family before starting the other.Nobody gets called back from the bar, and nobody waits through the other side's photos.
  3. Grandparents, small children and early leavers go first.They are the people for whom half an hour of standing actually costs something.

These are the rules the Family Formals planner follows when it orders a list. It weighs three things: how soon each person is finished, with people marked "needs to go first" counting much more; how many people walk in and out between photos; and how often the photographer switches families. Big groups first, in numbers

The common alternative is to build up: start with the couple and parents, who are already standing there after the ceremony, and add people. We put the same 13 photos in exactly the reverse order in the planner and compared.

Building up isn't slower for the photographer. It just keeps everyone in the biggest group waiting until the end, and in this family that's nine people, including the grandmother and a four-year-old.

Grandparents and small children early

"Needs to go first" is a single tick in the planner, and it matters most for people who are in more than one photo. Here's the same family ordered with and without those ticks:

Without the tick, Grandpa Lee's photo landed at the very end, and he would have stood through all of James's family photos to get there. With it, he sits down by 4:10. Ava happened to be early either way, because her photos are the big ones, but in families where a small child is only in a smaller group the tick moves them just as much.

Why the couple's parents go last

Parents are in more photos than anyone except the couple: six of thirteen each in the table above. Whatever the order, they're there for most of it. Putting their small photos ("Emma with parents", "Couple with James's parents") at the end costs nobody else anything, and those frames are calmer with the crowd gone.

The one photo that bridges the two families, "Couple with both sets of parents", works best in the middle: the first set of parents stays for it at the end of their side, and the second set is already gathered for their own photos.

Where the order usually goes wrong

  • Starting with whoever is nearest. Right after the ceremony the parents are next to you, so it feels natural to start with them. That's the build-up order, and the cousins wait the longest for it.
  • Going back to a side. Remembering "oh, we need one with Grandma" after you've moved on means calling her back from her seat. Put every photo on the list beforehand.
  • Waiting for someone missing. If someone isn't there when their photo comes up, skip it and come back. The rest of the list doesn't depend on it.

Questions

What order should wedding family photos go in?

Start with the biggest group on one side and take people away, finish that side, take the photo with both sets of parents, then do the other side the same way. Grandparents, small children and anyone leaving early should be done first.

Should the bride's or the groom's family go first?

Whichever side has more people who need to leave early, such as grandparents or small children. If it's even, start with the side whose extended family is larger, so more people are free sooner.

Is it faster to start with big groups?

Not faster overall: in our 13-photo example both orders took the same time and the same number of walk-ons. But starting big let the average relative go after 6.7 photos instead of 11.3, and only 3 people waited for the last photo instead of 9.

When should grandparents be photographed?

Early. Mark them as needing to go first and take their photos in the first few frames. In our example, doing that freed a grandfather after photo 5 instead of photo 13.
