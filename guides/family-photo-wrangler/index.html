Who should call names for family photos, and what to hand them

Pick one person per side who knows every face, isn't in many photos, and doesn't mind being loud. Give them a card for every photo that says who steps in, who steps out and who can leave.

Why the photographer shouldn't be the one calling names

Your photographer doesn't know which of the four women in blue is Aunt Sue. Reading names off a list and waiting for someone to answer is the slowest part of family photos, and it's the part a relative can do in half the time because they know every face. So most photographers ask the couple for one person on each side to gather people: the wrangler.

Who makes a good wrangler

  • Knows everyone on their side by sight, including the partners and the kids.
  • Is in few photos themselves. Not a parent or sibling of the couple: in a 16-person family we planned, each parent was in six of thirteen photos. A cousin, a family friend, an aunt by marriage or a bridesmaid who grew up with the family is ideal.
  • Is comfortable being loud and a bit bossy for half an hour, and polite while doing it.
  • Isn't needed somewhere else right after the ceremony: not the person doing a reading at the reception, not the best man.

In the example family, Cousin Nina is in one photo, the first. She's a natural fit for James's side: done after one frame, and she knows everybody. On Emma's side, Aunt Sue is in two photos, photos 3 and 4, and free after that.

If the family is small or one side has only a few people, one wrangler can cover both. If a parent is divorced, the wrangler should know that, and which parent is in which photo.

What to hand them: one card per photo

A list of photo names isn't enough. "Couple with Emma's immediate family" doesn't tell anyone who that is. The wrangler needs, for every photo, the names of the people in it, and more usefully, what changes from the photo before. The planner prints this as a call card for each photo:

  • In the photo: everyone in this frame, by name.
  • Step in: who joins compared with the last photo.
  • Step out: who leaves the frame but is needed again later, so shouldn't wander off.
  • Free to go: who is finished for good after this photo.
  • Call next: who to go and find now, while this photo is being taken.

Card 2 is the busy one: six people step out, Grandpa Lee is done, and eight people from the other family need to be standing there for card 3. Card 4 is the easy kind: one person swaps, two are free to go. In this 19-photo list, 9 cards carry a "call next" line, and those are the moments the wrangler earns their drink.

What they actually say

A script sounds silly until you're on a windy lawn with forty guests talking. Something like this works:

  1. Before the first photo"Family photos are starting. If you're on Emma's side and I call your name, stay close. Everyone else, the bar's over there."
  2. While a photo is being takenRead the "call next" line, quietly, to the people who need to come over: "Grandma June, Kate, Tom, Ava, Sue, Bob, Rick and Linda: you're next."
  3. After each photoRead "free to go" out loud: "Aunt Sue, Uncle Bob, you're done, thank you." Released people leave; unreleased people don't drift.
  4. When someone's missingTell the photographer to skip that photo, send someone to look, and come back to it. Don't hold the whole family.

Getting them ready

  • A week before: send them the list, or print the cards with the planner's "Print call cards" button. "Copy as text" gives the same run sheet for a phone or a group chat.
  • Walk through it once together. Ten minutes is enough to catch the names they don't know ("which one is Jordan?") and the people who need to go first.
  • Tell them who has special handling: a grandparent with a chair, a small child with a parent nearby, parents who shouldn't stand together.
  • On the day they stand next to the photographer, cards in hand, facing the guests, not behind the camera.

Give them a copy of the order, not just the list. If your photographer reorders the photos, print the cards again. The "step in" and "call next" lines depend on the order; a stale card sends people to the wrong frame.

Questions

Who should call out names for wedding family photos?

A relative or close family friend who knows everyone on that side by sight and isn't in many of the photos: a cousin, an aunt by marriage or a bridesmaid who grew up with the family. Not a parent or sibling of the couple, who are in most photos, and not the photographer.

Do we need a wrangler for each side of the family?

Usually yes, one per side, because each knows their own family's faces. For a small family, one person can cover both.

What should we give the person calling names?

A card for every photo with the names of who is in it, who steps in, who steps out, who is free to go and who to call next. The Family Formals planner prints these from your family list.

What does a wedding photo wrangler do during family photos?

They gather the next group while the current photo is being taken, tell people when they're free to go, and tell the photographer when someone is missing so that photo can be skipped and taken later.
