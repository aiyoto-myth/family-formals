The arithmetic

Family photo time is the number of photos times the minutes each one takes. That's all the planner does too: you set a start time and minutes per photo, and it gives you a time for every photo and a "done by" time for the last one. The hard part is the two inputs, so here are both.

How many minutes per photo

This is the number you have to choose. These are rules of thumb, not measurements; your photographer knows their own pace, so ask them.

  • 1 minute: small groups, everyone already standing nearby, a photographer who has done this many times and someone calling names.
  • 1.5 minutes: a sensible default for most weddings, and the planner's starting value.
  • 2 minutes: big groups, small children, or a grandparent who needs a chair brought in and out.
  • 3 minutes: family scattered across a venue with nobody gathering them. If that's your plan, the better fix is a wrangler, not a bigger number.

You can also mix. In the 16-relative family, the two "everyone on this side" photos are by far the biggest groups. Give those 3 minutes each and the other 11 photos 1.5 minutes, and you're at about 23 minutes instead of 20. That's a sum you do by hand; the planner uses one pace for every photo.

If cocktail hour runs 4:00 to 5:00 and the couple wants some of it, the first plan leaves them half an hour; the second, twenty minutes. Either way, the couple's own portraits come out of what's left, so tell your photographer the "done by" time when you plan the day.

Add a few minutes before the first photo. The planner's clock starts at your first photo. It doesn't know how long it takes to gather everyone after the ceremony. The first photo is the biggest group, so ask the officiant or DJ to announce it before guests scatter.

Where the minutes go

The clicking takes seconds. The rest is people moving. The planner counts every person who walks into or out of the frame between photos: 33 for the 13-photo list above, and 59 once the divorced pair is kept apart, because each split group means one parent out and another in.

Each of those moves is quick when the person already knows they're next, and slow when someone has to go and find them. That's why the planner prints a "call next" line on every card where new people are needed. In the 19-photo list, 9 of the 19 cards have one: the person calling names reads it out while the current photo is being taken.

How to make it shorter

  • Cut photos, not seconds. Each photo you untick saves the whole per-photo time. Cutting three optional photos at 1.5 minutes saves four or five minutes; rushing each pose saves far less and shows in the pictures.
  • Tell family their photos in advance. A text with "you're in photos 1 to 3, right after the ceremony, by the oak tree" removes the hunt.
  • Order the list so people leave as they finish. It doesn't shorten the total, but it shortens the wait for most people, which is what they'll remember. See the best order for family formals.

Questions

How long do wedding family photos take?

Count the photos and multiply by about 1.5 minutes each. In the Family Formals planner, a small family (parents and one sibling each side) came to 7 photos, about 11 minutes; a 16-person family to 13 photos, about 20 minutes; the same family with divorced parents kept apart to 19 photos, about 29 minutes.

How many minutes per family photo should we plan?

About 1.5 minutes is a sensible default. Plan 1 minute for small groups with someone calling names, and 2 minutes for big groups, small children or anyone who needs a chair. Ask your photographer about their own pace.

How much time should we set aside for family photos in the timeline?

Take the planner's done-by time and add a few minutes at the start to gather everyone after the ceremony. Starting at 4:10, a 13-photo list is done by about 4:30 at 1.5 minutes per photo.

Does a bigger family take longer?

Less than you'd think. A 28-relative family got the same 13 photos as a 16-relative one, because the extra aunts and cousins all join the one big group. Big groups take a bit longer to pose, so give them 2 or 3 minutes if you like.
