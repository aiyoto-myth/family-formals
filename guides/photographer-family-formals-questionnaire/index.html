The problem with "list your family groups"

Most questionnaires have a box that says "please list the family groupings you'd like". What comes back is a paragraph: "my side, then his side, both parents, grandma if she's up to it, and my dad and stepmom separately from my mom." You turn that into a shot list the night before, guess at the order, and on the day you read names off a phone while somebody's uncle asks if he's needed.

The fix isn't a longer form. It's asking for the family once, as people, and letting the list and the order come out of that.

The workflow

  1. Send the couple one link with your other pre-wedding questions.Free: send the planner's address and ask them to press "Send to your photographer" when they're done. Pro: press "Copy a blank link for your couple". The page they open says your studio asked them to fill it in, and the button at the end reads "Send back to" your studio name.
  2. The couple types in their family.Each person once, by side, with who they are (parent, step-parent, grandparent, sibling, sibling's partner, niece or nephew, aunt or uncle, cousin). They tick anyone who needs to go first and choose who never stands next to whom.
  3. They send it back.On a phone that's the share sheet; on a computer the link is copied with a one-line summary. The whole list lives inside the link. Nothing is uploaded, and neither of you needs an account.
  4. You open the link and check it.You see their list exactly as they left it: suggested photos, the order, a time for each. Untick what you don't shoot, add your own, move anything. Set your real start time and your pace.
  5. Print the call cards.One card per photo: who's in it, who steps in, who steps out, who is free to go, who to call next. Give them to whoever is calling names for each side.
  6. On the day, shoot.The wrangler reads the cards; you take the photos. You never need to ask "who's Jordan?"

The divorced-parent handling is the part that's hardest to get right from a paragraph of text. The couple says "keep my mom away from my dad and his wife" once; every group they'd share is split, and the two versions are put back to back so each swap is quick. More on that in divorced parents in family photos.

What the planner collects, so your form doesn't have to

  • The couple's names, and every relative on each side by name
  • How each person is related, which decides the suggested groups
  • Who needs to go first: a grandparent who can't stand long, a small child, someone leaving early
  • Who never stands next to whom
  • Extra photos they want that aren't on the usual list

Questions to keep on your own questionnaire

The planner only does the family list. These still belong on your form, and they're the ones couples forget:

  • Who is calling names on each side, and how you reach them on the day. If they say "you", point them to who should call names.
  • Anyone who uses a wheelchair or needs a chair, so you can plan the layout, not just the order.
  • Anyone who has died recently or isn't coming, and whether they want that acknowledged in any photo. You'd rather know than ask "where's Grandpa?"
  • Names that are hard to pronounce, spelled the way they sound.
  • The rain plan: where formals happen if the lawn is out.
  • Non-family groups (wedding party, friends) and whether they're during formals or later. Keeping them off the family list keeps the family part short.

Questions

What should a wedding family photo questionnaire ask?

Every relative on each side by name and how they're related, who needs to go first, who shouldn't stand together, and any extra groups. Also ask who will call names on each side, mobility needs, recently lost relatives, hard-to-pronounce names and the rain location.

Do couples need an account to fill it in?

No. They open the link, type in their family and press "Send to your photographer". The finished list travels inside the link they send you; nothing is uploaded.

Can I change the couple's list after they send it?

Yes. When you open their link you get the same editable list: untick photos, add your own, move photos up or down, or let the planner re-order them, then set your start time and pace.

What does Pro cost?

$19 once, not a subscription. It adds your studio name and logo to the printed cards, a blank link with your studio name for each couple, saved weddings and removes the "Made with" line. Everything else is free.
