How to fill it in

  1. Write both names at the top, and who calls names for each side.The wrangler is the person this sheet is really for.
  2. Cross out the photos you don't need.No grandparents coming? Strike that line. Siblings without partners or kids? The "immediate family" photo is the same as "parents and siblings", so cross one out.
  3. Write names, not roles, in every row."Aunt Sue, Uncle Bob", not "aunts and uncles". Whoever reads this sheet on the day may not know your family.
  4. Fill in the two boxes at the bottom.Anyone who needs to go first should be in the first photos of their side. Anyone who shouldn't stand together needs every shared group taken twice; see divorced parents.
  5. Add the times last.Start time, then add your minutes per photo for each row. At 2 minutes a photo, the full 13-photo list takes about 26 minutes. How long family photos take has more.

The rows are already in a sensible order: biggest group first, one family at a time, both sets of parents in between. The best order for family formals explains why.

The sheet fits on one page of US Letter or A4. Print it at 100% ("actual size"), not "fit to page".

Questions

Is there a free printable wedding family photo checklist?

Yes, this one. It prints on one page of Letter or A4, lists the usual 13 family photos in order, and has space to write the names for each photo, the time, who calls names and who shouldn't stand together.

What should be on a wedding family photo checklist?

For each side: everyone on that side, grandparents, immediate family, parents and siblings, parents, and the partner with their parents. Then one photo with both sets of parents, plus any extra photos that matter to you.

Should we give the checklist to the photographer or a family member?

Both. The photographer needs the list in advance; the relative calling names needs it on the day, because they know the faces.
