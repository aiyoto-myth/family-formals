      Wedding family photos, answered

      Who is included in the family photos at a wedding?

      Most couples photograph each side separately: first the whole extended family on one side (grandparents, aunts, uncles, cousins), then the immediate family (parents, brothers and sisters, their partners and kids), then just the parents. Then the same for the other side, and one photo with both sets of parents together. This planner suggests exactly that list from the people you enter, and you can untick or add any photo.

      What order should wedding family photos go in?

      Start with the biggest group and take people away, so each person is done as early as possible and can go and enjoy the party. Finish one family before starting the other, so people are not called back. Grandparents, small children and anyone leaving early should be finished first. The planner works this order out for you and shows, for every photo, who steps in, who steps out and who is free to go.

      How long do wedding family photos take?

      Plan about two minutes per photo once everyone is there; photographers who publish their numbers mostly allow two to three. Fifteen photos take roughly 30 to 45 minutes. Set your start time and minutes per photo and the planner shows when you will be done.

      How do you handle divorced parents in wedding photos?

      Add both parents, then keep them apart. The planner never puts them in the same photo; every group they would both be in is split into one photo with each of them, and a new partner can stand with the parent they came with.

      Who should call out names during family photos?

      Pick one relative who knows everyone on each side and give them the printed call cards. Each card says which photo is next, who steps in, who steps out and who can leave, so the photographer does not have to read names off a list.
